The **Network** screen models your radio side: the **towers** you broadcast from and the **sectors** (access points) on each tower that cover a direction or an area. It's the wireless counterpart to your **[fibre plant](/fibre-plant/)** — together they describe your whole physical network, whether you run radio, fibre, or both.

## Towers

A tower is a broadcast site. Give it a name and a location, and it appears on the **[map](/map/)** so you can see your coverage at a glance. A hybrid ISP runs towers and fibre points side by side on the same map.

## Sectors (access points)

Each tower carries one or more **sectors** — the radios that actually serve customers in a direction. Modelling sectors lets you:

- Attach each radio customer to the sector that feeds them.
- See how loaded a sector is and where you're running out of room.
- Know exactly who's affected when a sector or tower has a problem.

## Connecting customers to the network

A radio customer is linked to their **access point**; a fibre customer is linked to their **fibre point**. That link is what powers capacity tracking and fault impact — the system always knows what serves whom.

## On the map

Towers show as their own layer on the map. Combined with clients, routers, leads and fibre, you get one live picture of the network: where you cover, where customers are, and where demand is still unmet.
